- **Somatic comparison first:** Nora's Somatic Series is the only reportable non-homepage editorial
  GSC signal, United States Google Trends shows a strong five-year rise in relative interest, and
  current result pages often flatten the topic into a false binary.
- **Consultation guide second:** relative search interest has also risen sharply, the topic fits
  Nora's firsthand judgment, and the live first-call worksheet gives the article a useful next step.
- **No extra question-list article:** the worksheet already owns that task; another list would divide
  one reader need across two URLs.
- **No Minnesota somatic landing page yet:** local results are provider/service-led and the current
  verified inventory is not sufficient for a genuinely useful location page.

## The content pathway

1. Understand what a preliminary therapy conversation may be.
2. Optionally compare talk-centered and body-attuned care without creating a false binary.
3. Bring either path into a short set of practical questions.
4. Decide whether to book, keep comparing, or ask Healing Tides for help.

The visual direction is a calm editorial field guide. The signature interaction is a private call card built around one rule: **three questions are enough**. It contains no score, diagnosis, treatment recommendation, account requirement, or saved health information.

## What Nora needs to supply

One recorded 35–45 minute interview can complete both articles:

1. How do you distinguish a consultation, intake, and first session in your own work?
2. Do you offer a preliminary call? If so, what varies about its length, fee, or format?
3. What do you actually ask, and why?
4. What do you want someone to know they do not need to disclose?
5. What can a preliminary conversation reveal about fit—and what can it not reveal?
6. When do you decide a referral may be more appropriate?
7. How do you think about someone feeling uncertain after the first conversation?
8. What does “somatic” mean in your actual practice?
9. What are two real examples of body-attuned invitations you use?
10. How do you request and revisit consent?
11. Which named approaches are you actually trained to use?
12. When do you favor conversation, body awareness, integration, or referral?

Record and transcribe the interview. Nora should edit the resulting passages and approve the rendered final pages—not merely the interview transcript.

## Fifteen checks required to publish

1. Every health, legal, or insurance assertion has a directly supporting primary/official source or is clearly attributed to Nora’s experience.
2. Source population, credential, method, condition, and jurisdiction match the sentence.
3. Official links work on the final-review day and the page displays the source-check date.
4. The visible byline matches who actually created and owned the substance.
5. Nora confirms her real first-call process, questions, referral practice, and variability.
6. Nora defines only her actual use of “somatic” and verifies training, consent, boundaries, and referral points.
7. The somatic comparison makes the overlap and evidence limitations as prominent as the differences.
8. No literal trauma-storage/release/reset claim, diagnosis, universal exercise, or outcome guarantee appears.
9. The worksheet stores or sends no selections, requires no sensitive narrative, and has no practitioner score.
10. Minnesota credential links point to the correct board and do not imply Healing Tides performed verification.
11. Published/updated dates, sources, correction path, editorial method, and real reviewer are visible.
12. Title, H1, canonical, internal links, and topic boundary do not compete with the Twin Cities or cost guides.
13. JSON-LD exactly matches the visible author, reviewer, dates, headline, and canonical.
14. Keyboard, screen-reader, mobile, reduced-motion, copy, and print flows pass.
15. Nora signs off on the rendered final version.

## Automatic rejection conditions

The release fails immediately for an unsupported first-person Nora sentence, an invented practice or training claim, an overstated somatic mechanism or outcome, a generalized Minnesota legal rule, stored worksheet answers, a fit score, or schema that claims a review readers cannot see.
