## The two findings to lead with

Both are MN-specific and statute-verifiable. They're the reason this series can outrank national
templates — but read the second one's limits carefully; it was the one claim two researchers
disagreed on, and the loose version is wrong.

1. **Prior authorization for outpatient mental health is banned in Minnesota.** Minn. Stat.
   [62M.07](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62M.07) subd. 2(2): "No utilization review
   organization, health plan company, or claims administrator may conduct or require prior
   authorization of… outpatient mental health treatment or outpatient substance use disorder
   treatment, except for treatment which is a medication." Effective **2026-01-01** for plans
   offered/sold/issued/renewed on or after that date. VERIFIED against statute text by me directly.
   This one is clean and quotable.

2. **The standing-referral route to out-of-network care — real, but conditional.** Minn. Stat.
   [62Q.58](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.58) subd. 4(b): "If an enrollee receives
   services from a nonparticipating specialist because a participating specialist is not available,
   services must be provided at no additional cost to the enrollee beyond what the enrollee would
   otherwise pay for services received from a participating specialist."

   **⚠ Do not write this as "if your plan has no therapist, they must cover out-of-network."**
   Subd. 4 opens *"If a standing referral is authorized under subdivision 1 or is mandatory under
   subdivision 1a"* — the cost protection is **downstream of a granted standing referral**, and
   subd. 1 only applies *"if a referral is required"* by the plan at all. Subd. 1a's automatic
   triggers are: a chronic health condition · **a life-threatening mental or physical illness** ·
   pregnancy past the first trimester · a degenerative disease or disability · "any other condition
   or disease of sufficient seriousness and complexity to require treatment by a specialist."
   Ordinary depression or anxiety does **not** clear the life-threatening clause; it reaches the
   catch-all only if the plan agrees, through its own review. VERIFIED against statute text by me
   directly, after two researchers returned opposite conclusions (see *Provenance* at the bottom).

   **Honest framing for readers:** this is a lever to ask about **by name**, not a guarantee.
   "Ask whether you qualify for a standing referral under Minn. Stat. 62Q.58 — if one is granted and
   no participating specialist is available, the statute says an out-of-network specialist can't cost
   you more than an in-network one would." That sentence is defensible. Anything stronger isn't.

   Related and often confused: **62K.10** (network adequacy) provides **no** enrollee-level gap
   exception — its only remedy is a carrier waiver filed with MDH. So "gap exception" is a thing you
   *request from your plan as a courtesy*, not a Minnesota entitlement. Never call it a right.

Supporting: **Minn. Stat. [62Q.47](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.47)(g)** — mental
health therapy visits "shall be considered primary care visits for the purpose of applying any
enrollee cost-sharing," so therapy should hit the *primary-care* copay tier, not specialist.
VERIFIED twice, independently. Also **[62Q.56](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.56)** —
continuity of care, up to 120 days when a provider leaves the network.

## Out-of-network / superbills

**⚠ Lead with the MN reality, not the mechanics.** MNsure's own words: HMOs "don't cover health
services out-of-network (except in an emergency)"; EPOs "only cover health services if you use
doctors… in the plan's network (except in an emergency)."
[VERIFIED — MNsure](https://www.mnsure.org/shop-compare/about-plans/plan-types-costs/index.jsp).
MDH licenses Blue Plus, HealthPartners, Medica, Quartz and UCare as **HMOs**
[VERIFIED — MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/insurance/managedcare/planinfo/hmo.html).
For a large share of MNsure readers **a superbill is worth nothing** — say that first, before
explaining how superbills work. **Do NOT claim Blue Plus (or anyone) is "the PPO option on MNsure"**
— that traces only to affiliate blogs. UNCERTAIN.

**Superbill must carry:** patient name/DOB/insurance ID · provider name, credentials, **NPI**,
**Tax ID/EIN**, practice address · date(s) of service · **CPT** per session · **ICD-10** diagnosis ·
fee per unit · **amount paid** · balance · provider signature. It mirrors the CMS-1500 fields.
Note honestly that a superbill is an **industry artifact, not a regulated form** — there is no
primary CMS definition of one.

**CPT:** 90791 diagnostic eval · **90832** 30-min (16–37) · **90834** 45-min (38–52) · **90837**
60-min (53+) [VERIFIED — [APA Services](https://www.apaservices.org/practice/reimbursement/health-codes/psychotherapy)].
**Place of service: POS 02 = telehealth, patient NOT at home; POS 10 = patient at home**
[VERIFIED — [CMS POS code set](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/place-of-service-codes/code-sets)].
Modifier 95 audio-video / 93 audio-only — UNCERTAIN (CMS PDF blocked; verify wording). [CHANGES ANNUALLY]

**The mechanics — reimbursement is a % of the plan's ALLOWED amount, not of what you paid.**
Allowed amount = "the maximum amount a plan will pay for a covered health care service"
[[healthcare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/allowed-amount/)]. OON coinsurance = "the
percentage… you pay **of the allowed amount**"
[[healthcare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/out-of-network-coinsurance/)] ← the
load-bearing citation. Sequence: OON deductible → plan sets the allowed amount → coinsurance % of
*allowed* → **you eat the gap**. All VERIFIED.

**⚠ Never publish a typical OON reimbursement percentage.** No MN insurer SBC was obtainable; the
commonly-repeated "50–70%" traces only to marketing pages. UNCERTAIN.

**The No Surprises Act does NOT cover voluntary out-of-network therapy** — it covers emergencies and
OON providers *at in-network facilities*; CMS explicitly excludes "some doctors' offices"
[VERIFIED — [CMS key protections](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/nsa-keyprotections.pdf)].
Minnesota's parallel, [62Q.556](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.556), is likewise
surprise-billing-only. **What self-pay clients DO get:** a written **Good Faith Estimate**, with
dispute rights if billed **≥$400 over** [VERIFIED —
[CMS GFE FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/faqs-good-faith-estimate-uninsured-self-pay-part-5.pdf)].

**⚠ Timely filing is the trap nobody warns about.** HealthPartners individual/family: submit "within
90 days," hard cutoff 15 months [VERIFIED —
[claims policy PDF](https://www.healthpartners.com/content/dam/brand-identity/pdfs/plan/ind-fam-claims-payment-policies.pdf)].
Medica provider-side 180 days. BCBSMN's form says only "the timeframe specified by your contract."
**Practical advice: submit monthly, don't save them up.**

### The phone script (read verbatim to member services; get a reference number)

1. Is this plan an HMO, EPO, PPO or POS? 2. Do I have **any** out-of-network outpatient mental
health benefit, or is OON covered only for emergencies? *(If none — stop. A superbill is useless.)*
3. What's my OON deductible, and how much is met today? 4. After it, what's my OON coinsurance
percentage? 5. **What is the allowed amount you use for CPT 90837 and 90834 in my ZIP?** *(This, not
the percentage, determines the check.)* 6. Is that based on UCR or a percentage of Medicare — which?
7. Is telehealth covered OON — POS 10 and POS 02, modifier 95? Modifier 93 audio-only? 8. Any annual
visit limit? Any prior authorization for outpatient therapy? *(Note: banned as of 2026-01-01.)*
9. Does OON cost-sharing count toward my out-of-pocket maximum? 10. How do I submit, and which form
number? 11. **What's the timely-filing deadline from date of service?** 12. Reference number for this
call, and your name?

## Rights that matter

- **Federal MHPAEA** parity — financial requirements, quantitative limits, NQTLs. **⚠ The 2024 final
  rule is under a non-enforcement policy announced 2025-05-15** pending *ERIC v. DOL* + 18 months;
  the statute and 2013 rule remain enforceable. **Recheck at publish and every update.** VERIFIED
- **MN parity:** 62Q.47 (above) and [62Q.53](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.53)
  (no stricter "medically necessary" definition for mental health)
- **MN Commerce has enforced it** — HealthPartners **$150,000** (2023-05), Medica **$300,000**
  (2023-07), UnitedHealthcare **$450,000** (2024-05). All VERIFIED, all citable. Proof that appealing works
- **Where to complain:** fully-insured non-HMO → MN Commerce 800-657-3602 · HMO → MDH Managed Care
  800-657-3916 · self-funded employer plan → US DOL EBSA 1-866-444-3272
- **External review** ([62Q.73](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.73)): file within
  **6 months**, **binding on the plan but not on you**, **cost borne by the plan** — the $25 fee was
  eliminated 2024-08-01. **⚠ The MN AG handbook still says $25; it's stale — don't cite it**
- **Network adequacy:** Minn. Stat. **62K.10** (⚠ *not* 62K.14 — that's pediatric dental, a common
  error). MDH mental-health standard: lesser of **30 miles or 30 minutes**
- **No Surprises Act good-faith estimate:** uninsured/self-pay have a right to a written GFE within
  **3 business days** of scheduling; if billed **≥$400 over**, dispute within **120 days**. Note
  honestly that NSA balance-billing protections **don't** cover routine outpatient therapy
- **Telehealth** ([62A.673](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62A.673)): coverage + payment
  parity, cost-sharing no greater than in-person. **⚠ Audio-only counts as telehealth only until
  2027-07-01** — hard sunset, date the article

## Never publish these three

Called out because each is plausible, widely repeated, and wrong:

1. **Any specific out-of-network coinsurance percentage** for MN mental health. Unsourceable.
2. **That a "gap exception" is a Minnesota legal right.** It isn't — see the 62Q.58 / 62K.10
   treatment above. It's a request you make of your plan.
3. **That a PPO with out-of-network benefits is available on MNsure.** Unverified, and the
   marketplace is HMO-dominated.

## Provenance — how this was built, and one thing that nearly went wrong

Two independent research passes ran against primary sources (MN Revisor, DHS, MDH, MNsure, CMS,
healthcare.gov, and practices' own published rate pages). Where they agreed, the claim is marked
VERIFIED. Where a source blocked automated fetching or only secondary sources existed, it's UNCERTAIN
and gated below.

**They disagreed on exactly one thing, and it was the headline.** Pass A reported Minn. Stat.
62Q.58 subd. 4 as a general "network-gap right" — if your plan has no available in-network therapist,
you may see an out-of-network one at in-network cost — and called it the most useful under-known
right for Minnesotans. Pass B reported that no such right exists in Minnesota. Both cited real
statutes.

I pulled 62Q.58 directly. **Pass A quoted subd. 4(b) accurately but dropped its opening condition**:
the protection applies only where a standing referral is authorized (subd. 1, at the plan's
discretion, and only "if a referral is required") or mandatory (subd. 1a, whose mental-health trigger
is a *life-threatening* illness). Pass B was right that it isn't a general entitlement, and right
that 62K.10 offers enrollees no gap remedy — but wrong that the cost-protection language doesn't
exist. The corrected treatment above is what the statute actually supports.

**The lesson worth keeping:** a confident, well-cited, quotable finding was still wrong in the way
that matters — it would have told a reader in a mental-health crisis that they had a right they
don't have, under a licensed clinician's byline. Every statutory claim in anything built from this
gets read against the primary source before it publishes. Agent agreement is not verification.
