## Verdicts

**what-therapy-costs-in-minnesota**

> Not yet safe to hand a licensed clinician. The empirical spine is genuinely clean — every MDH percentage, all six MHCP fee-schedule rates, the Commerce filing, the DHS premium table, CUHCC's tier amounts, Open Path's rates and the private-practice fees match their sources exactly, and there is no fabricated experience anywhere — but seven load-bearing items fail: an invented same-day service at Wilder, a misquote of CUHCC, the wrong statutory standard for the uninsured good-faith estimate under 62J.81, a truncated 62Q.53 subd. 2 definition offered as an appeal standard, a telehealth statute stretched to a population it expressly excludes, and a "no source found" caveat contradicted by 62Q.47(g), which the draft itself cites.

**medical-assistance-therapy-coverage-minnesota**

> No — not safe to hand a clinician as-is, because of one finding that will actively mislead a reader in the most vulnerable position the article addresses: it tells undocumented adults they are a MinnesotaCare eligibility category, citing § 256L.04, subd. 10(b), without subd. 10(c), which made adult undocumented noncitizens ineligible beginning January 1, 2026. The statutory work is otherwise unusually careful — I verified roughly forty direct quotations against primary text and all but two are verbatim with correct elisions — but there are three more dropped conditions (§ 256L.03's child/pregnancy full-benefit overrides, § 256B.761's two-part federal-approval trigger, § 245I.10, subd. 2(f)'s "or an update"), a citation to a repealed subdivision, an undisclosed pending repealer sitting under every CCBHC quote, and four population-level "most people" claims with nothing behind them.

**waiting-for-therapy-minnesota**

> No — not safe to hand a licensed clinician for signature yet. The draft is unusually disciplined about sourcing and its ⚠ CONFIRM notes are honest, and I verified nearly every hour, phone number, dollar threshold and volume figure as exact; but four load-bearing legal claims are wrong in ways that would misdirect a distressed reader — a dropped internal-appeal prerequisite, a telehealth-parity statute that by its own terms does not apply to the Medical Assistance and MinnesotaCare readers this page is written for, a mis-stated medical-necessity comparator, and an unconditional "you can complain to MDH" that is actually the HMO-only process.

**first-call-with-a-therapist**

> Not safe to hand a licensed clinician as written. Two defects would actively mislead a distressed reader: the draft asserts that no Minnesota duty-to-warn provision was located for social workers when Minn. Stat. 148E.240 subd. 6 imports 148.975 wholesale into the byline clinician's own license, and it states an unconditional 30-day right to your own records while omitting the mental-health withholding exception in 144.292 subd. 7. Its citation discipline is otherwise unusually good — quoted text is verbatim, the CMS $400/3-day figures and the MDH 988 and board-phone details check out, no lived experience is fabricated, and the [NORA] holes are genuinely left open — but one pointer is wrong (2150.7570 should be 2150.7575), the "one hard number" is repealed law, and six conditional rights read as unconditional.

**telehealth-therapy-minnesota**

> The citation work is genuinely excellent — I fetched all 10 linked sources and every quoted passage in the piece matches the source verbatim, every subdivision pointer is correct (3(b),(c),(d),(e),(f); 4(b),(c),(d); 5(a),(d); 8; 2(c),(h); 62A.011 subd 2 and 3(10); 256B.0625 subd 3b(a),(c),(d),(e)(1),(e)(2) and 46; 256L.03 subd 1(a)), and it survives the superseded-law trap: I confirmed 62A.673 was not amended in the 2026 session at all, that 256B.0625's 2026 amendments touched subds 4, 5m, 8, 17, 17b, 18i, 20, 47, 77, 78 but NOT 3b, and that 2026's HF 2435 — which would have pushed both audio-only dates to July 1, 2028 — died in conference. It is not publishable as-is, though: the "short version" section strips the scheduled-appointment/standard-of-care condition off audio-only so a conditional coverage right reads as unconditional, and one sentence asserts a categorical exclusion of out-of-state therapists that the cited statute does not establish and that Minnesota's now-operational Counseling Compact cuts against.

### what-therapy-costs-in-minnesota

**[CRITICAL]** `[Wilder Foundation in St. Paul](https://www.wilder.org/services/mental-health/) commits in writing that "no one will be denied access to our services due to inability to pay," and offers walk-in menta`

INVENTED DETAIL. The quote is verbatim, but the Wilder page never says "evaluations." Under the heading "Drop In to Get Started" it says only: "Clients aged 6 and above can come to the Wilder Center Clinic to get started without an appointment. Monday - Thursday 9:00 a.m. – 3:00 p.m." The only same-day assessment language on the page belongs to a different program — "Wilder Recovery Services... comprehensive assessments for substance use" — and has different hours (it adds Fridays 9:00 a.m. – 2:00 p.m.). A reader in distress could travel to St. Paul expecting a same-day diagnostic evaluation and receive an intake instead.

*Fix:* Replace "offers walk-in mental health evaluations" with the page's own framing: "lets clients aged 6 and above drop in to get started without an appointment, Monday through Thursday, 9am–3pm."

**[CRITICAL]** `- **If you have no coverage,** a provider must give you, at no cost and within **ten business days** of a complete request, a good-faith estimate of the allowable payment the provider has agreed to ac`

CONDITION DROPPED / WRONG STANDARD in the one section written as an action a reader can take. Minn. Stat. 62J.81, subd. 1(a) gives the uninsured a different and weaker thing: "a good faith estimate of the average allowable reimbursement the provider accepts as payment from private third-party payers for the services specified by the consumer and the estimated amount the noncovered consumer will be required to pay." "[T]he allowable payment the provider has agreed to accept" is the *first* sentence of subd. 1(a) — the clause for consumers who DO have coverage ("the allowable payment the provider has agreed to accept from the consumer's health plan company"). The draft has swapped the two standards. It also mis-splits the section: subd. 1(a) obliges providers to estimate for insured consumers too, so an insured reader is told the provider route is not theirs.

*Fix:* Rewrite the bullet to the statute's own words — "a good-faith estimate of the average allowable reimbursement the provider accepts from private third-party payers, and the estimated amount you will be required to pay" — and change the bullet headers so the provider duty is not presented as belonging only to the uninsured.

**[MAJOR]** `Subdivision 2's definition is care "appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, level, setting, and duration, to the enrollee's diagnosis or condition."`

CONDITION DROPPED, and the draft then tells readers to build an appeal on it: "An appeal should argue that the plan's mental health definition is more restrictive than the subdivision 2 standard." The quoted fragment is verbatim but is only the first clause. Subd. 2 continues: "Medically necessary care must be consistent with generally accepted practice parameters as determined by health care providers in the same or similar general specialty as typically manages the condition, procedure, or treatment at issue and must: (1) help restore or maintain the enrollee's health; or (2) prevent deterioration of the enrollee's condition." The omitted practice-parameters clause is the part payers actually litigate. A reader appealing on the partial standard is appealing on a floor lower than the one the statute sets.

*Fix:* Add the rest of subd. 2 after the quoted clause, or cut the quotation and say only that the benchmark is the full definition at subdivision 2, which the reader should read in full before appealing.

**[MAJOR]** `CUHCC writes: "CUHCC will accept any amount that you can pay and bill you for the rest."`

MISQUOTE. The page reads: "If you cannot pay the full amount at the time of service, CUHCC will take any payment that you can make and will bill you for the rest." Nine of the quoted words differ from the source. Under a licensed clinician's byline, quotation marks around a payment commitment a clinic did not write is the kind of error that costs the byline its credibility even though the meaning survives.

*Fix:* Use the source wording verbatim: "CUHCC will take any payment that you can make and will bill you for the rest."

**[MAJOR]** `⚠ re-check: a scheduled statutory sunset with direct access consequences for rural and low-income Minnesotans unless the Legislature extends it.`

NON-APPLICABLE SCOPE. Minn. Stat. 62A.673, subd. 8 states: "This section does not apply to coverage provided to state public health care program enrollees under chapter 256B or 256L." Low-income Minnesotans on MA or MinnesotaCare — the population the article has just spent two sections addressing — are expressly outside this statute; their telehealth coverage runs through 256B.0625, subd. 3b. Relatedly, "Minnesota's telehealth definition includes audio-only communication only until July 1, 2027" is a definition "for purposes of this section" only, and it is conditional: audio-only counts only "if the communication is a scheduled appointment and the standard of care for that particular service can be met through the use of audio-only communication," or, for mental health and SUD, if the enrollee initiated it in an emergency or crisis when a scheduled appointment was not possible.

*Fix:* Cut "and low-income" from the re-check note, and change "Minnesota's telehealth definition" to "the commercial-coverage telehealth definition in that section" with the scheduled-appointment / standard-of-care condition stated.

**[MAJOR]** `⚠ CONFIRM: whether Minnesota commercial plans typically apply the deductible rather than a flat copay to psychotherapy. This is plan-specific and no aggregate Minnesota source was found.`

The answer is in a statute the draft cites four paragraphs earlier. Minn. Stat. 62Q.47, paragraph (g): "Regardless of the health care provider's professional license, if the service provided is consistent with the provider's scope of practice and the health plan company's credentialing and contracting provisions, mental health therapy visits and medication maintenance visits shall be considered primary care visits for the purpose of applying any enrollee cost-sharing requirements imposed under the enrollee's health plan." Telling a reader no source exists, when Minnesota law gives them a specific lever against being charged specialist cost-sharing, is the most consequential omission in the commercial-insurance section.

*Fix:* Replace the ⚠ CONFIRM with 62Q.47(g), quoted, plus its conditions (scope of practice; the plan's credentialing and contracting provisions).

**[MAJOR]** `- **If you are insured,** you can require your **health plan** to give you a good-faith estimate of the allowable amount and your out-of-pocket share, also within ten business days of a complete reque`

CONDITION DROPPED. Minn. Stat. 62J.81, subd. 1a(a) limits the duty to "a specified provider within the network" and to "an enrollee intending to receive specific health care services." As written, a reader could believe they can compel an estimate for an out-of-network therapist — the exact situation in which someone shopping for a therapist most wants a number, and the one the statute does not cover.

*Fix:* Insert the qualifier: "...for a specific service from a specific provider in your plan's network."

**[MINOR]** `Most Minnesotans looking for a therapist are trying to answer one question — *what will this cost me* — and the public information available to them is thin.`

UNSOURCED EMPIRICAL CLAIM about a population, in the article's first sentence. Nothing behind "Most." The draft's own checklist forbids exactly this elsewhere.

*Fix:* Delete "Most" and the population claim: "For anyone looking for a therapist in Minnesota, one question comes first — what will this cost me — and the public information is thin."

**[MINOR]** `The assessment figures matter on their own: an evaluation is a separate and larger cost than a therapy hour, and it is easy to miss when you are budgeting for "therapy."`

Generalizes provider pricing from a single practice's fee page. The one other source in the article supports it only marginally: the MHCP table shows 90791 at $144.44 against 90837 at $139.17, a $5.27 gap. The draft's own front matter says sentences that "generalized from one source" were deleted; this one survived.

*Fix:* Attribute it: "At this practice the evaluation costs more than a therapy hour ($292 vs. $225) — worth noting because an evaluation is a separate line item from the sessions that follow."

**[MINOR]** `a group practice in **Rockford, Minnesota** (Wright County, about 35 miles west of Minneapolis)`

INVENTED DETAIL — the parenthetical is not on the cited fee page, which gives only the address (8060 State Highway 55 Suite 100, Rockford, MN 55373). Rockford is ~27 miles by road and ~24 straight-line from Minneapolis, and northwest rather than west; the city lies mainly in Wright County but extends into Hennepin.

*Fix:* Delete the parenthetical, or reduce it to "about 30 miles northwest of Minneapolis."

**[MINOR]** `MinnesotaCare does carry cost-sharing — a monthly deductible, copays for non-preventive visits, ER, eyeglasses and drugs`

Two qualifiers dropped from DHS EPM 3.1.2.2. The page's copay is for "Copays for nonemergency ER visits," not ER visits generally — a reader could delay a genuine emergency to avoid a copay that does not apply. The whole list is also scoped on that page to "Adults age 21 or older."

*Fix:* Write "copays for non-preventive visits, nonemergency ER visits, eyeglasses and drugs — for adults 21 and older."

**[MINOR]** `while therapists in private practice offer sliding fees "on a very limited basis"`

The Augsburg page says: "Some therapists in private practice will also provide services on a 'sliding fee scale' basis that adjusts according to your income (on a very limited basis)." Dropping "Some" turns a statement about some therapists into a statement about the profession.

*Fix:* Restore the hedge: "while some private-practice therapists offer sliding fees 'on a very limited basis.'"

**[MINOR]** `seven days a week, 9 AM to 1 AM: 651.288.0400 or 855-WARMLINE, text 85511`

Incomplete instruction on a crisis-adjacent resource. The Warmline page directs users to text "Support" to 85511; the keyword is omitted. A reader who texts something else may not reach peer support.

**[MINOR]** `**Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics (CCBHCs)** — [Minn. Stat. 245.735](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.735) requires a CCBHC to "establish fees for clinic services..."`

PENDING LAW, undisclosed. ### medical-assistance-therapy-coverage-minnesota

**[CRITICAL]** `and people ineligible for MA based on immigration status with income at or below 200 percent (subd. 10(b))`

CONDITION DROPPED, and the worst kind — a conditional right presented as unconditional to the population least able to absorb a wrong answer. § 256L.04, subd. 10(c) reads: "For purposes of this subdivision, enrollment in MinnesotaCare for undocumented noncitizens who are age 18 years old or older is limited to those enrolled as of June 15, 2025. Notwithstanding paragraphs (a) and (b), undocumented noncitizens who are age 18 years old or older are ineligible for MinnesotaCare beginning January 1, 2026." That date has passed. An undocumented adult reading this article today is told they have a coverage path they do not have, on the strength of a subdivision that paragraph (c) expressly overrides by name.

*Fix:* Delete the clause. If the path is kept for enrolled-before-June-15-2025 adults and for minors, it must carry subd. 10(c) verbatim in the same sentence — not as a footnote.

CONDITION DROPPED. § 256L.03, subd. 1a: "Children are eligible for coverage of all services that are eligible for reimbursement under the medical assistance program according to chapter 256B, except special education services..." and subd. 1b: "A pregnant woman enrolled in MinnesotaCare is eligible for coverage of all services provided under the medical assistance program according to chapter 256B retroactive to the date of conception." The subd. 1 carve-out list the article quotes correctly does not reach child or pregnant enrollees. A parent on MinnesotaCare reading this concludes their child cannot get case management. *Fix:* Add after the sentence: "Those carve-outs apply to adult enrollees; § 256L.03, subds. 1a and 1b give children and pregnant enrollees the full chapter 256B benefit set."

**[MAJOR]** `Certification is voluntary — [§ 245.735](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.735) frames its requirements as things "[e]ntities that choose to be CCBHCs must" do`

SUPERSEDED/PENDING LAW, undisclosed. Every CCBHC quotation in this section comes from § 245.735, subd. 3 — which the Revisor's page flags as repealed by Laws 2026, ch. 121, art. 7, s. 55. Article 7, s. 56: "This article is effective January 1, 2028." CONDITION DROPPED. **[MAJOR]** `On the face of the text, a therapy hour is not a copay-triggering visit.`

The reading is overbroad against the text it rests on. The visit definition the draft itself quotes includes "physician or physician assistant" and "advanced practice nurse." A psychiatrist is a physician, and a psychiatric clinical nurse specialist or nurse practitioner is an advanced practice nurse — and both are "mental health professionals" who deliver psychotherapy under § 245I.04, subd. 2(1) and (4). Psychotherapy delivered by either would be a copay-triggering visit on the face of the same text. The ⚠ CONFIRM flag mitigates but does not cure a stated conclusion that is wrong for a real slice of therapy visits.

*Fix:* Replace the sentence with: "On the face of the text, a therapy hour billed by a psychologist, clinical social worker, LMFT or LPCC is not a copay-triggering visit; therapy billed by a psychiatrist or a psychiatric advanced practice nurse would be."

**[MAJOR]** `Psychotherapy is covered outright, with one condition attached to who delivers it`

CONDITION DROPPED. The quoted sentence sits in § 256B.0671, subd. 11(b), whose first sentence conditions the whole thing: "'Psychotherapy' means treatment of a client with mental illness that applies to the most appropriate psychological, psychiatric, psychosocial, or interpersonal method that conforms to prevailing community standards of professional practice to meet the mental health needs of the client." Subd. 11(a) is also broader than quoted — "Medical assistance covers psychotherapy for a client." Telling a reader the only condition is the provider's credential is wrong in the same paragraph the quote comes from, and the article's own later point that "Coverage is diagnosis-driven, not distress-driven" contradicts it.

*Fix:* Change to: "Psychotherapy is covered, with conditions in the same paragraph — the client must have a mental illness, the method must conform to prevailing community standards, and a mental health professional or clinical trainee must deliver it." Cite subd. 11(b), not the bare section.
