# What therapy actually costs in Minnesota

Most Minnesotans looking for a therapist are trying to answer one question — *what will this cost me* — and the public information available to them is thin.

Cost keeps Minnesotans out of care at a measurable rate. In 2023, **7.0% of Minnesotans went without mental health care because of cost**, up from 6.1% in 2021 and roughly level with 7.2% in 2019, according to the [Minnesota Department of Health's Health Access Survey chartbook](https://www.health.state.mn.us/data/economics/chartbook/summaries/section6summaries.html). Nearly a quarter of Minnesotans (**24.5%**) went without some form of health care due to cost that year, and among the uninsured it was **52.7%** ([MDH, 2023 Health Access Survey](https://www.health.state.mn.us/data/economics/hasurvey/docs/mnha2023.pdf)). ⚠ re-check: the MDH survey runs on a two-year cycle; the next release would update the 2023 figures.

This piece replaces guesswork with figures that have sources — and says so plainly where a figure does not exist.

## What Minnesota publishes itself: the Medicaid fee schedule

The prices the state of Minnesota puts in public are Medicaid maximums — the most the state will allow for each service. From the [MHCP fee schedule](https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/mhcp-fee-schedule_tcm1053-294225.pdf), effective 02/01/26:

| Service | CPT | MN Medicaid maximum allowable |
|---|---|---|
| Intake diagnostic evaluation | 90791 | **$144.44** |
| Psychotherapy, 60 min | 90837 | **$139.17** |
| Psychotherapy, 45 min | 90834 | **$94.54** |
| Family therapy, patient present | 90847 | **$90.65** |
| Psychotherapy, 30 min | 90832 | **$71.52** |
| Group psychotherapy | 90853 | **$24.95** |

Two honest caveats. DHS defines that column as "the current maximum allowable for the procedure," and states that the listed amounts **do not include the legislative decreases or increases** — so it is not necessarily what a clinic is finally paid. And this is the Medicaid rate. It is not a commercial rate and not a self-pay price; presenting it as either would be wrong.

There is no companion table for commercial insurance or for cash pay. If you see a "statewide average price for therapy in Minnesota," ask where it came from. ⚠ re-check: the fee schedule is versioned and dated (this one reads "AS OF 07/10/2026"); confirm the effective date before publication and at each legislative session.

## What self-pay looks like when a practice publishes it

Some Minnesota practices post their rates. One that does, a group practice in **Rockford, Minnesota** (Wright County, about 35 miles west of Minneapolis), lists **$225 for a 55-minute session** with a master's-level therapist, **$167 for 45 minutes**, and **$126 for 30 minutes**; a mental health evaluation is **$292**, and ADHD/autism assessment starts at **$512** ([Transformational Therapy, fee page](https://transformationaltherapymn.com/about-us/our-fees/)). ⚠ re-check: one private practice's posted rates, subject to change without notice; verify annually or drop the specific numbers.

The assessment figures matter on their own: an evaluation is a separate and larger cost than a therapy hour, and it is easy to miss when you are budgeting for "therapy."

## What "sliding scale" does — and does not — mean

"Sliding scale" is not a price. It is a promise that a price exists somewhere below the posted rate, and the terms vary from one organization to the next.

At the Rockford practice above, the sliding-scale fee is a flat **$60 for a 55-minute session — with a master's-level intern**, not a licensed therapist, and the page adds a condition: "You will need to demonstrate financial hardship in order to utilize services at these rates." That is one shape a sliding scale can take — a lower fee attached to a supervised trainee, plus a means test. It is not the only shape, and no source supports calling it typical.

Some Minnesota organizations put real numbers on the page. [CUHCC in Minneapolis](https://cuhcc.umn.edu/patient-care-services/payment-options) publishes its mental health visit fee by discount level: **$30 (A), $45 (B), $60 (C), $75 (D), $90 (E)**, and no discount at level F. CUHCC also provides **no-cost therapy and psychiatry to uninsured Hennepin County residents**. Answering its own question — what if you cannot pay the full amount at the time of service — CUHCC writes: "CUHCC will accept any amount that you can pay and bill you for the rest." Two administrative traps are worth writing down: the discount runs from your application date only if you "provide the proof of income within 45 days," and enrollment lasts one year from the application date unless your income changes.

Others do not publish amounts. [Hamm Clinic in St. Paul](https://hammclinic.org/clients/) states on its clients page that it serves the uninsured on a sliding fee scale based on household size and income, with no dollar figures on that page. [Minnesota Community Care](https://www.mncare.org/cost/sliding-fee-discount/) ties its discount to the federal poverty guidelines, charges an unspecified nominal fee up front, and requires re-application every six months. ⚠ CONFIRM: widely circulated third-party figures for Hamm Clinic (a "$15 session" and a "$4,000 deductible" trigger) appear only on directory sites and are not on Hamm's own page. Do not print them without calling the clinic.

[Wilder Foundation in St. Paul](https://www.wilder.org/services/mental-health/) commits in writing that "no one will be denied access to our services due to inability to pay," and offers walk-in mental health evaluations Monday through Thursday, 9am–3pm, for ages 6 and up.

Augsburg University publishes a low-cost therapy referral list that is candid on both ends: most listed agencies slide "in some cases down to $0," while therapists in private practice offer sliding fees "on a very limited basis" ([Augsburg CWC](https://www.augsburg.edu/cwc/counseling/counseling-low-cost/)). ⚠ re-check: a university referral list, updated at the school's discretion.

## If you have Medical Assistance or MinnesotaCare

**Medical Assistance (Medicaid).** As of January 1, 2024, Minnesota law states the MA benefit plan "must not include cost-sharing or deductibles for any medical assistance recipient or benefit" ([Minn. Stat. 256B.0631](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256B.0631)). DHS's own eligibility policy manual agrees: MA enrollees "do not have deductibles, copays or co-insurance," though some owe premiums, spenddowns, waiver obligations or parental fees ([DHS EPM 2.1.1.2.3](https://hcopub.dhs.state.mn.us/epm/2_1_1_2_3.htm)). So a therapy visit on MA has no copay by statute.

Note a live contradiction on a state website: MNsure's consumer page says MA members "have small co-pays for some services, usually $1 – $3" ([MNsure](https://www.mnsure.org/financial-help/ma-mncare/)). That conflicts with both the statute and DHS policy. ⚠ re-check: the live MNsure page is bot-protected and could not be read directly; this quote was confirmed from a 2026 archived snapshot of that page. Re-read the live page before publication, and if MNsure has corrected it, rewrite this paragraph rather than leaving a stale accusation.

**MinnesotaCare.** MinnesotaCare does carry cost-sharing — a monthly deductible, copays for non-preventive visits, ER, eyeglasses and drugs — but DHS states flatly: "There is no cost sharing for mental health services" ([DHS EPM 3.1.2.2](https://hcopub.dhs.state.mn.us/epm/3_1_2_2.htm)). By statute there is also no copay, coinsurance or deductible for mobile crisis intervention or crisis assessment, and cost-sharing never applies to children under 21 or to American Indians as federally defined ([Minn. Stat. 256L.03](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256L.03)). ⚠ CONFIRM: the actual MinnesotaCare deductible and office-visit copay amounts — the DHS page carrying them is bot-blocked and could not be read.

If you cannot pay a MinnesotaCare copay at the visit, DHS is explicit that providers must still serve you that day, though a provider "does not have to serve an enrollee again if their cost sharing is still not paid."

Premiums, for 2026 coverage, top out at **$80 per person per month**, reached at 200% of the federal poverty guideline ($31,300/year for a household of one). The lowest rung above zero is **$4/person/month** (35–54% FPG), rising to **$12** at 100–109% and **$25** at 140–149%. Four groups owe **$0**: households with combined annual income below 35% FPG, people under age 21, American Indians and Alaska Natives and their family members, and — in the table's own words — "military members who have completed a tour of active duty within 24 months of approval for MinnesotaCare and their family members (for up to 12 months)" ([DHS-4139A premium table](https://edocs.dhs.state.mn.us/lfserver/Public/DHS-4139A-ENG)). ⚠ re-check: this table is effective January 1 – December 31, 2026 and is reissued annually. ⚠ CONFIRM: MA and MinnesotaCare income limits by household size — the MNsure page carrying them served a CAPTCHA and could not be verified.

## If you have commercial insurance

Having a card is not the same as affording care. **19.7% of privately insured Minnesotans** said in 2023 they were not very or not at all confident they could pay their deductible ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/data/economics/hasurvey/docs/mnha2023.pdf)), and forgone care ran to **29.7%** among people on individual-market coverage. Minnesota Commerce approved an average **21.47% individual-market premium increase for plan year 2026**, ranging by carrier from 7.40% to 30.76%: Medica Insurance Company 30.76%, UCare 27.48%, HealthPartners Insurance Company 19.51%, Blue Plus 18.70%, HealthPartners Inc. 13.31%, Quartz Health Plan MN 7.40% ([MN Commerce 2026 fact sheet](https://mn.gov/commerce-stat/insurance/rates/2026/2025_health_insurance_fact_sheet_individual.pdf)). ⚠ re-check: annual filing; superseded each fall.

Two Minnesota statutes are worth knowing by number, and worth stating precisely, because a denial appeal turns on the exact standard.

**[Minn. Stat. 62Q.47](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.47) — cost-sharing parity.** Plans may not place a greater financial burden on outpatient mental health cost-sharing than on outpatient medical, and the same rule applies to inpatient psychiatric and residential treatment. This one *is* a mental-health-versus-medical comparison.

**[Minn. Stat. 62Q.53](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.53) — a floor under "medically necessary."** A health plan covering mental health services may not require those services to satisfy a definition of "medically necessary care," "medical necessity," or similar term "that is more restrictive with respect to mental health than the definition provided in subdivision 2." Subdivision 2's definition is care "appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, level, setting, and duration, to the enrollee's diagnosis or condition."

Read that second one carefully, because it is easy to get backwards — draft 1 of this article did. **62Q.53 does not require a plan to apply the same medical-necessity definition to mental health that it applies to physical health.** The benchmark is the statutory definition in subdivision 2, not the plan's medical-side definition. An appeal should argue that the plan's mental health definition is more restrictive than the subdivision 2 standard.

⚠ CONFIRM: whether Minnesota commercial plans typically apply the deductible rather than a flat copay to psychotherapy. This is plan-specific and no aggregate Minnesota source was found.

## You can require a price in writing — unless you are on MA or MinnesotaCare

Start with the exclusion, because it is easy to miss. [Minn. Stat. 62J.81](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62J.81) defines its terms so that, for purposes of that section, "consumer" **does not include a medical assistance or MinnesotaCare enrollee, for services covered under those programs**. If you are on MA or MinnesotaCare, this section is not your tool; the no-cost-sharing rules above are.

For everyone else:

- **If you have no coverage,** a provider must give you, at no cost and within **ten business days** of a complete request, a good-faith estimate of the allowable payment the provider has agreed to accept, and the amount you will be required to pay. The provider must also disclose other fees or charges you may be required to pay, such as facility fees.
- **If you are insured,** you can require your **health plan** to give you a good-faith estimate of the allowable amount and your out-of-pocket share, also within ten business days of a complete request.
- **Either way, the estimate is not a promise.** The statute itself says the estimate does not constitute a legally binding estimate of the allowable charge for, or cost to the consumer of, the services.

Federally, the No Surprises Act adds a good-faith-estimate right for people not using insurance. CMS states: "Usually, if you don't have or use health insurance, providers must give you a good faith estimate of what your care will cost," when you schedule care at least three business days in advance or ask for one — and "You may be able to dispute your bill if it's at least **$400 more** than the estimate" ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/nosurprises/consumers/understanding-costs-in-advance)). If you are using your insurance, this is not the right you are looking for; the Minnesota health-plan estimate above is.

## Teletherapy and cost

Minnesota requires commercial payment parity for telehealth: the same rate as in person, and cost-sharing that is "not in addition to, and does not exceed" the in-person copay for the same service ([Minn. Stat. 62A.673](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62A.673)). One date to put on the calendar: Minnesota's telehealth definition includes **audio-only** communication only **until July 1, 2027**. ⚠ re-check: a scheduled statutory sunset with direct access consequences for rural and low-income Minnesotans unless the Legislature extends it.

## The genuinely low-cost routes

- **[Walk-In Counseling Center](https://walkin.org/get-counseling-now/)**, 2421 Chicago Ave, Minneapolis — "completely free and anonymous"; no appointment during clinics and no insurance needed. Clinic hours: **Mon/Wed/Fri 1–3pm for both in-person and virtual** (phone or login) services, and **Mon–Thu 5:30–7:30pm virtual only**. ⚠ re-check: hours change; confirm before publishing.
- **CUHCC** — no-cost therapy and psychiatry for uninsured Hennepin County residents; published discount tiers otherwise (see above).
- **Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics (CCBHCs)** — [Minn. Stat. 245.735](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.735) requires a CCBHC to "establish fees for clinic services for individuals who are not enrolled in medical assistance using a sliding fee scale that ensures that services to patients are not denied or limited due to an individual's inability to pay for services." Note the scope: that clause is written for people **not** on medical assistance. ⚠ CONFIRM: a current list of Minnesota CCBHC sites — the DHS program pages are bot-blocked.
- **School-linked behavioral health** — grantees "shall serve students regardless of health coverage status or ability to pay" ([Minn. Stat. 245.4901](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.4901)). ⚠ CONFIRM: current grantee site list.
- **[Open Path Collective](https://openpathcollective.org/pricing-and-eligibility-for-affordable-therapy/)** — $40–70 per individual session, $40–80 couples/family, $30 flat with student interns, plus a one-time $65 membership. Means-tested: household income under $100,000 and no adequate mental health coverage. Rates assume a 50-minute session and can be prorated up (Open Path's own example: $126 for a 90-minute intake). Their own guidance is framed around affordability, not a bright line: "If you find that your co-pay is greater than $70 and you can't afford that amount, or that you have an extremely high annual deductible to meet before counseling is covered, you can consider" Open Path. ⚠ re-check: Open Path is a national (US/Canada) nonprofit, not a Minnesota program — it sets these rates nationally and has changed them before. See the scope note in the checklist below.
- **A Twin Cities couples practice** publishes a **$30 per session** low-cost option staffed by supervised marriage and family therapy interns ([MN Couple Therapy](https://www.mncoupletherapy.com/rates-insurance)). ⚠ re-check.
- **[NAMI Minnesota support groups](https://namimn.org/support/support-groups/)** — free of charge, statewide, for people with mental illness and for families. Not clinical care, and not a substitute for it, but zero cost and real.
- **[Minnesota Warmline](https://mentalhealthmn.org/what-we-do/peer-support/minnesota-warmline/)** — calls "are answered by peer support staff who have first-hand experience living with a mental health condition," seven days a week, 9 AM to 1 AM: 651.288.0400 or 855-WARMLINE, text 85511. ⚠ CONFIRM: the Warmline's page does not state a cost; do not describe it as free until verified. ⚠ re-check hours.

## One cost that never appears on the bill's face

Minnesota imposes a **1.8% MinnesotaCare provider tax** on health care providers' gross revenues ([Minn. Stat. 295.52](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/295.52)). It does not sit behind every fee: [Minn. Stat. 295.53](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/295.53) subd. 1(b) exempts, among other categories, payments received for services provided under the Medicare program, payments received for home health care services, and payments received from the behavioral health fund under chapter 254B. Where the tax does apply, it is one of the costs a fee has to cover.

On whether it can appear on your bill, the same section speaks directly: a provider "must not state the tax obligation under section 295.52 in a deceptive or misleading manner," and must not "separately state tax obligations on bills provided to patients, consumers, or other payers when the amount received for the services or goods is not subject to tax" (295.53 subd. 3). Minn. Stat. 295.582 separately addresses transferring the tax to third-party purchasers. ⚠ CONFIRM: whether a separate 1.8% patient line item is permitted on services that *are* taxable — no Commerce or Attorney General interpretation on that narrower question was located. Do not write "your therapist can add 1.8% to your bill."
