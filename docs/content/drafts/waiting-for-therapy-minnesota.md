# What to do while you are waiting for a therapist in Minnesota

You called a clinic, or six clinics, and got voicemail, or an intake line, or a date six weeks out. That gap is real and it is not a sign that you asked wrong.

This page is about the gap itself: what a waitlist in Minnesota actually is, what is open to you today at no cost, and the specific things you can say to be seen sooner. It is a resource list, not treatment.

## If today is the hard part

Waiting does not mean you have nothing until the appointment.

**988** is open 24 hours a day, seven days a week, by call or text ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/people/mentalhealth.html)). MDH lists online chat as a third way to reach it ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/suicide/988/systems.html)). Minnesota area codes are answered by in-state centers, including First Call, FirstLink, Greater Twin Cities United Way, and Mental Health Minnesota, with assigned county coverage ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/suicide/988/systems.html)). *⚠ re-check: the roster of Minnesota Lifeline centers and their county coverage can change; re-verify against the MDH 988 systems page annually.* *⚠ CONFIRM: MDH publishes no chat-specific schedule. Do not add hours for chat.*

Four things people ask before they call, answered by MDH's own FAQ:

- You do not need insurance. The service is free.
- You do not need ID or documentation, and you do not have to give personal information.
- It is not only for suicide. MDH says you can contact 988 for a tough day, feeling overwhelmed, or alcohol and drug concerns.
- It rarely brings police or an ambulance: fewer than 2% of Lifeline contacts are connected to emergency services.

All four: [MDH 988 questions](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/suicide/988/questions.html). One practical limit from the same page — calls and texts to 988 require active paid phone service, monthly or prepaid.

**If you are not in crisis but the day is heavy,** the [Minnesota Warmline](https://mentalhealthmn.org/what-we-do/peer-support/minnesota-warmline/) is peer support, seven days a week, 9 AM to 1 AM, at 651-288-0400 or 855-WARMLINE, or text "Support" to 85511. It is answered by staff with their own lived experience of a mental health condition, and they can connect you straight to crisis services if that turns out to be what you need. *⚠ CONFIRM: the operator's page says nothing about cost. Do not write "free" for this line unless Mental Health Minnesota's own page says so.* Overnight, MDH lists the [Minnesota Peer Support Connection Warmline](https://www.health.state.mn.us/people/mentalhealth.html) at 844-739-6369, 5 p.m. to 9 a.m.; its operator, [Wellness in the Woods](https://mnwitw.org/mnwarmline), describes it as for adults living in Minnesota, answered by a trained peer specialist, with texting possibly available 5 p.m. to 1 a.m. *⚠ re-check: warmline hours and phone numbers change with funding cycles. Confirm both lines before publish and every six months after.*

**By text:** Minnesotans can message "MN" to 741741 to reach Minnesota's 24/7 crisis text line ([FastTrackerMN](https://fasttrackermn.org/)); [Crisis Text Line](https://www.crisistextline.org/text-us/) describes its own support as free, 24/7, and confidential. *⚠ CONFIRM: the "MN" keyword is sourced to an organization page, not a state page. Verify with MDH or Crisis Text Line directly before publishing.*

## What a waitlist in Minnesota actually is

A waitlist is usually two different things stacked together: a clinic's capacity, and your health plan's network.

At a **Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic (CCBHC)**, the first phone call is not just a name on a list. State law requires a preliminary screening and risk assessment at the *first contact*, and that screening determines how urgent your need is ([Minn. Stat. 245.735](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.735)). The same statute gives the clinic up to 60 calendar days after that screening to finish the full comprehensive evaluation — which is part of why the wait can feel long even when nothing has gone wrong. *⚠ re-check: 245.735 is amended in most sessions; re-read subdivision text each year.*

Two things this means for you in practice. First, if something changes while you are waiting, that is information the clinic's screening was designed to use — call back and say so plainly. Second, CCBHCs must tell every client at the start of care about the full array of services available under the CCBHC model ([245.735](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.735)), so "please tell me everything available here while I wait for a therapist" is a request the clinic is required to answer.

On the insurance side, Minnesota law requires health plan networks to include enough mental health and substance use providers that covered services are available **without unreasonable delay** ([Minn. Stat. 62K.10](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62K.10); [MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/insurance/managedcare/networkadequacy/index.html)). Appointment waiting times are one of the criteria the Commissioner of Health may weigh in judging whether a network is adequate, and MDH enforces the section ([62K.10](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62K.10)). So a long wait can be a regulatory question, not only bad luck.

What the law does **not** do is give you a number. There is no statutory maximum wait for routine outpatient mental health care in Minnesota. If you read anywhere that the law requires an appointment within X days, that is not accurate.

*⚠ CONFIRM before publish: no Minnesota-specific average or median wait time for a first therapy appointment exists in any primary source. Do not add one, even from a news story.*

## Doors that are open without an appointment

**[Walk-In Counseling Center](https://walkin.org/)**, 2421 Chicago Avenue, Minneapolis: counseling with no appointment, no cost, and no insurance, offered in person and virtually. Published clinic hours are Monday, Wednesday, and Friday 1:00–3:00 PM (virtual and in person) and Monday–Thursday 5:30–7:30 PM (virtual only). *⚠ re-check: clinic hours are the most volatile item on this page. Verify on walkin.org the week of publish.* *⚠ CONFIRM: the site says "virtual" and does not name a platform. Do not write Zoom.*

**[Ramsey County Mental Health Urgent Care](https://www.ramseycountymn.gov/residents/health-medical/clinics-services/mental-health-crisis-services/mental-health-urgent-care)**, 402 University Avenue East, St. Paul: walk-in access to psychiatrists, social workers, and trained peer support staff, as an alternative to an emergency room. Published hours are Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–7 p.m. and Saturday–Sunday 10 a.m.–5 p.m., serving youth, teens, and adults. On cost, the county says it will bill your insurance for crisis services and that if the service is not fully covered, or you are uninsured, you will not have to pay any remaining cost. *⚠ re-check: hours and address, annually.*

**County mobile crisis.** Minnesota law requires every county board to provide or contract for enough mental health emergency services to meet local need, and states plainly that clients must not be charged ([Minn. Stat. 245.469](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.469)). The same statute requires immediate direct access to a mental health professional during business hours and toll-free phone access to a professional or practitioner evenings, weekends, and holidays. Medicaid-funded mobile crisis providers must have screening, assessment, and intervention available 24/7 in their service area ([Minn. Stat. 256B.0624](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256B.0624)).

Worth knowing: the Medicaid rules define a "mental health crisis" broadly. They do not require imminent danger — only a situation that, without crisis services, would likely significantly reduce your functioning in daily life or lead to a more restrictive setting ([256B.0624](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256B.0624)).

Stat. 62A.673](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62A.673)). *⚠ re-check: audio-only (phone) appointments count as covered telehealth only until July 1, 2027 under the current text of 62A.673. 3. **"Can someone do the screening now, even if the therapy appointment is later?"** At a CCBHC that screening is mandatory at first contact and sets your acuity ([245.735](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.735)).
4. **"What is available to me here while I wait?"** CCBHCs must be open evenings and weekends and have crisis management available 24 hours a day ([245.735](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.735)).
5. **"Would peer support be covered for me now?"** Medical Assistance covers mental health certified peer specialist services for people receiving crisis, adult rehabilitative, or intensive residential treatment services ([Minn. Stat. 256B.0615](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256B.0615)).
6. **If you call back with a change,** say what changed and when. Ask them to note it on your file.

## If money is the reason the wait is long

Minnesota CCBHCs must use a sliding fee scale for people not enrolled in Medical Assistance, set up so that services are **not denied or limited because someone cannot pay** ([245.735](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.735)). Separately, Minnesota's designated Essential Community Providers that qualify on nonprofit status must charge on an income-based sliding fee schedule and give unrestricted access regardless of a client's financial limitations; MDH maintains the public [ECP list](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/insurance/managedcare/ecp/index.html).

If you are uninsured, MDH advises asking each clinic specifically whether it offers a sliding fee scale, a payment schedule, or accepts public assistance or MinnesotaCare. The same page lists **Portico HealthNet** (651-489-2273 or 1-866-489-4899) for referrals to community clinics for primary care, prenatal care, reproductive health, and dental care — not mental health specifically ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/insurance/clearinghouse/resources.html)).

On coverage: Medical Assistance income for adults without children is at or below 133% of federal poverty guidelines, with an additional 5% FPG disregard ([DHS Health Care Programs Manual](https://hcopub.dhs.state.mn.us/epm/2_2_3_3.htm)). MinnesotaCare covers people at or below 200% FPG who are not eligible for MA ([DHS](https://hcopub.dhs.state.mn.us/epm/3_3_2.htm)). *⚠ re-check: FPG thresholds are reissued annually and new guidelines take effect each July 1 ([DHS](https://hcopub.dhs.state.mn.us/epm/2_2_3_3.htm)). Any dollar figure added here goes stale every July.* *⚠ CONFIRM: MNsure enrollment dates and special-enrollment windows were not verifiable; do not publish any date.*

Do not publish clinic-specific sliding-scale dollar amounts. None were verifiable.

## If your plan is the bottleneck

Minnesota parity rules say benefits for mental health and substance use services cannot be more restrictive than benefits for physical health, including on cost sharing, visit limits, and prior authorization ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/insurance/managedcare/faq/parity.html)). A plan may not apply a definition of "medically necessary" that is more restrictive for mental health than for physical health ([Minn. Stat. 62Q.53](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.53)). If a plan denies payment, it must give you the reason in writing, and it must disclose the criteria it uses to decide whether a treatment is covered ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/insurance/managedcare/faq/parity.html)).

**You can file a complaint because your access to providers is limited.** MDH says so directly, and there is no cost to file ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/insurance/managedcare/complaint/investigate.html)). Complaints go in by phone at 651-201-5100 or 1-800-657-3916, by form, or through an online portal with no login required, and can run at the same time as an internal complaint to your plan. A normal investigation may take up to 45 days.

If care was **denied**, a Minnesota HMO denial can go to external appeal through MDH within six months of the date of the adverse determination. A normal external appeal takes up to 45 days, and you can request an **expedited 72-hour** review if a 45-day wait could harm your health — have a provider fill out the expedited section of the form ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/insurance/managedcare/complaint/external.html)). Which regulator handles your appeal depends on your coverage: DHS for Medicaid plans, CMS for Medicare, the U.S. Department of Labor for self-insured plans, MDH for Minnesota HMOs, and the Minnesota Department of Commerce for some others ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/insurance/managedcare/complaint/external.html)).

MDH also publishes the right phone number by coverage type: DHS Ombudsman for Public Managed Health Care Programs 651-431-2660 for public program enrollees; Commerce 651-539-1600 or 800-657-3602 for individual or small group insurance; MDH 651-201-5100 or 1-800-657-3916 for individual or small group HMOs ([MDH parity FAQ](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/insurance/managedcare/faq/parity.html)). *⚠ re-check: agency phone numbers and the parity FAQ, annually.*

One note on the law, so nobody overstates it:

- Minnesota's own numeric travel-distance standard was repealed in 2025; time and distance now defer to the federal standard at 45 CFR 155.1050 ([62K.10](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62K.10)). *⚠ re-check: re-read the published section text each session before publish.*

Separately, enrollees have a statutory right to available and accessible emergency services 24 hours a day, seven days a week, covered in or out of network, and denial review must weigh what a reasonable layperson would have believed about whether care could wait for the next available clinic appointment ([Minn. Stat. 62Q.55](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.55)).
