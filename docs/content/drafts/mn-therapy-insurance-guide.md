## Paying for therapy in Minnesota: what your insurance actually owes you

Most people find out how their mental-health coverage works at the worst possible moment — mid-search,
already worn down, holding a list of names that don't call back. This is the explanation I wish
everyone had before they started.

Two things changed in Minnesota recently that almost nobody has been told about. Both are in your
favour.

### The thing that changed on January 1st

**Your plan can no longer require prior authorization for outpatient mental health treatment.**

That's not a guideline. It's Minnesota law — [Minn. Stat. 62M.07](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62M.07),
subdivision 2, which says no utilization review organization, health plan company, or claims
administrator may require prior authorization of outpatient mental health treatment or outpatient
substance use disorder treatment. The one exception is medication.

It applies to plans offered, sold, issued or renewed on or after **January 1, 2026** — so if your plan
renewed this year, it applies to you. If someone at your insurer tells you a course of therapy needs
to be pre-approved, you can name the statute.

> ⚠ CONFIRM: whether each named payer has updated its published prior-auth policy to comply. Don't
> name a specific insurer as non-compliant without checking.

### Therapy is a primary-care visit, for what you pay

[Minn. Stat. 62Q.47](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.47) says mental health therapy
visits "shall be considered primary care visits for the purpose of applying any enrollee cost-sharing."

In practice: your therapy copay should be your **primary-care** copay, not the higher specialist one.
Whether a deductible applies first still depends on how your plan is built — on a high-deductible plan,
it will. But if you're being charged a specialist rate for therapy, that's worth a phone call.

### The one people get wrong — including some websites

There's a Minnesota provision worth asking about by name, and it's widely described online in a way
that overstates it. Here is the accurate version.

[Minn. Stat. 62Q.58](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.58), subdivision 4, says that if you
receive services from a non-participating specialist *because a participating one wasn't available*,
those services "must be provided at no additional cost to the enrollee beyond what the enrollee would
otherwise pay" in network.

That's real. But it only kicks in **after a standing referral has been granted** — either approved by
your plan under subdivision 1, or automatic under subdivision 1a. The automatic list covers a chronic
health condition, a *life-threatening* mental or physical illness, pregnancy past the first trimester,
a degenerative disease or disability, or any other condition serious and complex enough to require a
specialist. Everyday anxiety or depression doesn't clear the life-threatening bar; it reaches that last
catch-all clause only if your plan agrees.

**So it is a lever to ask about, not a guarantee.** The sentence that's worth saying on the phone:
*"Can I be evaluated for a standing referral under Minnesota Statute 62Q.58? If one is granted and you
have no participating specialist available, the statute says an out-of-network specialist can't cost me
more than an in-network one."*

What you should *not* be told, and what I'd rather you hear from me first: a "gap exception" is **not**
a Minnesota entitlement. The network-adequacy statute gives you no personal remedy — its only mechanism
is a waiver the carrier files with the Department of Health. Asking is still worth it. Just know you're
asking a favour, not claiming a right.

### If you're thinking about paying out of pocket

Start here, because it saves people a lot of wasted effort: **most plans sold on MNsure are HMOs**, and
in MNsure's own words, HMOs don't cover services out of network except in an emergency. If that's your
plan, a superbill is worth nothing and no amount of paperwork changes that. Find out before you build a
filing system.

If you *do* have out-of-network benefits, the mechanic that surprises people is this: **you're
reimbursed a percentage of what your plan decides the service is worth, not a percentage of what you
paid.** Your insurer sets an "allowed amount." Your coinsurance is a percentage of that. The difference
between your therapist's fee and the allowed amount is simply yours.

`[NORA — optional: how you explain this to clients who ask. One short paragraph.]`

**A superbill** — the itemised receipt you submit yourself — needs your name and date of birth, the
therapist's name, credentials, NPI and tax ID, the practice address, each date of service, the CPT code
per session, an ICD-10 diagnosis code, the fee, what you paid, and a signature.

Two practical warnings. First, that diagnosis code is not optional — claiming reimbursement means a
diagnosis goes to your insurer. For some people that matters, and it's a reason some choose to pay cash.
Second, **don't save your superbills up.** Filing deadlines are real and they're shorter than people
expect — one Minnesota plan asks for claims within 90 days. Submit monthly.

> ⚠ CONFIRM before publishing: modifier 93/95 wording against a current CMS source. POS 02 (telehealth,
> not at home) and POS 10 (telehealth, at home) are verified.

**A note on "surprise billing" protections:** they don't apply here. The federal No Surprises Act covers
emergencies and out-of-network providers working inside in-network facilities. Choosing an
out-of-network therapist is a voluntary choice, and it isn't covered. What you *do* get, if you're
uninsured or self-paying, is a written **Good Faith Estimate** before you start — and if the final bill
comes in $400 or more above it, you have a route to dispute.

### What therapy actually costs here

Minnesota practices that publish their rates are mostly in the same band: **roughly $160 to $250 for a
50–60 minute session, with about $200 being typical.** First appointments — the longer diagnostic
intake — run higher, commonly $190 to $300.

Two honest caveats. Rates outside the Twin Cities are hard to survey because fewer practices publish
them, so **don't assume greater Minnesota is cheaper.** And sliding scales, while common, are usually
limited in number, income-documented, and often full. Some practices don't offer one at all and say so
plainly.

The genuinely lower-cost path is usually a pre-licensed clinician or supervised intern — the same
practice charging $225 may offer $60 with an intern, and some training clinics go lower still.

> ⚠ CONFIRM annually: all rate figures. They move.

### If money is the barrier right now

These are real Minnesota options, not a national list:

- **[Walk-In Counseling Center](https://www.walkin.org/)**, 2421 Chicago Ave S, Minneapolis — free,
  no appointment, no insurance, and you may stay anonymous. In-person and virtual hours; check the site
  rather than trusting any schedule printed elsewhere.
- **[Open Path Collective](https://openpathcollective.org/pricing-and-eligibility-for-affordable-therapy/)**
  — a one-time $65 lifetime membership, then roughly $40–70 a session, or about $30 with a student
  intern. Household income under $100,000.
- **[CUHCC](https://cuhcc.umn.edu/patient-care-services/payment-options)** (University of Minnesota) —
  sliding fee, mental-health visits starting around $30.
- **University training clinics** — the [U of St. Thomas Interprofessional Center](https://www.stthomas.edu/interprofessional-center/counseling-services/)
  and [MSU Moorhead](https://www.mnstate.edu/academics/graduate/counseling/counseling-services) both
  provide counselling free of charge.
- **Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics** are required by
  [Minn. Stat. 245.735](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.735) to use a sliding scale so
  services "are not denied or limited due to an individual's inability to pay."
- **County access:** Hennepin Front Door **612-348-4111** · Ramsey **651-266-7899**.
- **[NAMI Minnesota](https://namimn.org/support/information-and-resources/nami-helpline/)** —
  free support groups and classes; helpline 651-645-2948 x117. Not a crisis line, and calls come back
  in about a week.

> ⚠ CONFIRM: the current CCBHC list (the only fetchable DHS list is from 2020).

One correction worth making, because outdated lists still circulate: **Argosy's Twin Cities campus
closed in 2019** and no clinic replaced it. Saint Mary's, St. Cloud State, Bethel, Capella and Adler
don't run public clinics either.

And a caution about the therapist-matching services people are often pointed toward — Headway, Grow,
Rula, Alma. They are **insurance-first directories, not low-cost care.** Rula's published cash rate is
$150 a session. They can be useful; they're not the affordable option people assume.

### If you're on Medical Assistance

Cost-sharing for Medical Assistance is **$0 by statute**
([Minn. Stat. 256B.0631](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256B.0631)), and MinnesotaCare
carries no cost-sharing for mental health services. You'll need a diagnostic assessment before ongoing
psychotherapy, though up to three sessions can happen first.

The hard part isn't the coverage, it's finding someone. I'm not going to give you a statistic about how
many Minnesota therapists accept MA, because no honest source measures it. What I can tell you is why
it's difficult: the state's own data puts what it pays for a 60-minute session well below what the same
hour bills at privately. That gap is the whole explanation.

> ⚠ CONFIRM: 2026 rates — a 2025 law moved professional rates to at least 100% of Medicare effective
> 2026-01-01, so the older published figures may be stale. Do not print a dollar figure until checked.
> ⚠ Eligibility thresholds reset every July 1.

Note also that the state's provider directory covers fee-for-service members only. If you're in a
managed-care plan, use your plan's directory — and call to confirm either way.

### When they say no

`[NORA — this is the section that most needs you. What you tell someone who's been denied. What you've
seen work. 2–4 sentences.]`

Appeals work more often than people expect, and Minnesota regulators have enforced this. The Department
of Commerce has issued parity consent orders against three major insurers in recent years, with
penalties from $150,000 to $450,000. That's not a guarantee about your claim. It's evidence that the
rules have teeth.

If an internal appeal fails, Minnesota offers **external review**
([Minn. Stat. 62Q.73](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/62Q.73)): file within six months, the
plan pays for it, and the decision binds the insurer but not you.

> ⚠ CONFIRM: the Attorney General's handbook still lists a $25 filing fee. That fee was eliminated in
> 2024 — don't cite the handbook figure.

Where to complain, depending on your plan: fully-insured non-HMO → **Commerce, 800-657-3602** · HMO →
**Health, 800-657-3916** · a self-funded employer plan → **US Department of Labor, 866-444-3272**.

Federal parity law is in an unsettled moment — a 2024 rule is under a non-enforcement policy pending
litigation — but the underlying statute still applies, as does Minnesota's own parity law.

> ⚠ CONFIRM at publish **and at every update**: the federal parity enforcement posture. This is the
> most time-sensitive fact in the piece.

### Before you make the call

Ask for "outpatient behavioral health," not "therapy" — it routes you better. Get a reference number
before you hang up.

1. Is this plan an HMO, EPO, PPO or POS?
2. Do I have any out-of-network outpatient mental health benefit, or only for emergencies?
   *(If none, stop — a superbill won't help you.)*
3. What's my out-of-network deductible, and how much is met?
4. After that, what's my coinsurance percentage?
5. **What allowed amount do you use for CPT 90837 and 90834 in my ZIP code?** *(This, not the
   percentage, decides your reimbursement.)*
6. Is telehealth covered — place of service 10 and 02?
7. Any annual visit limit? Any prior authorization for outpatient therapy? *(Note: prohibited as of
   January 1, 2026.)*
8. Does out-of-network cost-sharing count toward my out-of-pocket maximum?
9. How do I submit, and what's the filing deadline from the date of service?
